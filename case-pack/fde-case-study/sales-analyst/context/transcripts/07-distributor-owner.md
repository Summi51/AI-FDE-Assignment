# Interview: Distributor Owner, Southern Brazil

- **Role:** Owner and managing director of an independent agri-inputs distributor
- **Region:** Brazil South, north of Rio Grande do Sul (buys from the company in territory BR-S01)
- **Date:** 10 October 2026, in person at the distributor's warehouse
- **Interviewer:** Riklr FDE
- **Note:** Conducted in English with some Portuguese. The owner is not a company employee. The regional sales manager arranged the visit but did not attend.

---

**Distributor Owner:** Come, come. Mind the forklift. We talk in the office, it's quieter. You want a chimarrão? No? Coffee then. Everybody from the city says no to the chimarrão.

**Interviewer:** Coffee is great, thank you. Can you tell me about the business?

**Distributor Owner:** My father started it with one truck. Now we have three warehouses, eleven trucks, forty people. We sell seed, crop protection, some fertiliser, some biologicals. About ninety dealers buy from us, cooperatives too, and some big farmers direct. Your company is one of my suppliers. An important one. Not the only one. I have four, five suppliers depending on the product.

**Interviewer:** How do you choose between suppliers?

**Distributor Owner:** Terms and service. The products are similar, honestly, the farmer does not see big differences in a fungicide. So I look at three things. How many days to pay. Does the credit note come when they promised. And the rep. Does the rep pick up the phone when I have a problem, or only when he has a target.

**Interviewer:** Let's talk about last season. What happened?

**Distributor Owner:** Okay. [Sighs.] August last year, 2025. The territory manager comes with his regional manager. Big visit. They bring a presentation on a tablet, the new scheme for the season. Early order discount, extra days on payment if I order before end of September, and a volume bonus on top. The message was clear. "We need the volume now, the season will be good, the forecasts are fine." And the season before was okay, so I believed it.

**Interviewer:** How big was the order compared with a normal year?

**Distributor Owner:** Big. Maybe half again what I usually take in that window. Mostly fungicide and soybean seed, some insecticide. I said to my son, this is a lot. He said, with these terms, we pay in March after the harvest, what is the risk? That is how everyone thinks in August.

**Interviewer:** Was there pressure, or was it a good deal?

**Distributor Owner:** Both. It was a good deal on paper. But the territory manager was, how to say, very motivated. He called three times in September. "Can you take two more trucks, it closes my year." I like him, he is a good boy, so I helped. Every supplier does this at the end of their year. Yours ends in September, so September is when they come.

**Interviewer:** When did you first see a problem?

**Distributor Owner:** Before the rain problem. That is what people at the company don't understand. October, November, my dealers stopped reordering. Normally in October a dealer calls every week, "send me twenty more boxes". Last year, they called and asked about price. Or they did not call. The farmers were buying less, waiting, cash was tight after a weak year for soy prices. Then the rain failed in November and December and it was finished.

**Interviewer:** How did you know dealers were slowing down?

**Distributor Owner:** My sales people talk to the dealers every day. And I see the orders. In October I looked at the reorder book and said, this is not normal. Also one dealer in my town, a friend, said to me "my shelf is full of your fungicide, I can't take more until Christmas." When a friend says that, you know.

**Interviewer:** Did you tell the company?

**Distributor Owner:** I told the territory manager in November. Here, in this office. He said "calma, it will move when the spraying starts." His regional manager said the same on the phone. They had their own numbers, I suppose. They looked at what I bought from them. They did not look at what I sold. Nobody asks me what I sold.

**Interviewer:** Does the company see what your dealers sell to farmers?

**Distributor Owner:** Some. Your company has the points program. The dealers scan the boxes when they sell to the farmer and they get points, a small bonus. Some of my dealers like it, they scan everything. Others never bother. I don't know what the company does with it. I never saw a report from it. It's for them, not for me.

*[A warehouse worker comes in with a question about a truck. The owner answers in Portuguese and returns after about a minute.]*

**Distributor Owner:** Sorry. Loading for tomorrow. Where were we?

**Interviewer:** The stock that wasn't moving.

**Distributor Owner:** Right. So January, I walk in the warehouse and it is full. Pallets to the roof with your logo. The plastic still on. My son made a spreadsheet, how many months to sell this at the current pace. It said two seasons for some products. Fungicide expires. I can't wait two seasons.

**Interviewer:** What happened with the return?

**Distributor Owner:** A fight. A polite fight, but a fight. In March I called the regional manager directly. Not the territory manager, the boy was in a difficult position. I said I need to send back. He came here himself, I respect that. He walked the warehouse with me and he was embarrassed. I was embarrassed. Fifteen years I know him.

**Interviewer:** Why was it a fight if you had return rights?

**Distributor Owner:** The contract says I can return. But then the details. Which lots. Which products. Only full boxes. Only before a date. Then they wanted to give me a credit instead of taking the product back, a credit against next season's order. So I would have to buy again to get my money. I said no. In the end three trucks went back in March, and some smaller loads in April. Close to a quarter of what I took for the season.

**Interviewer:** How did it end?

**Distributor Owner:** They took it. The credit for the returns came. But the scheme money from the season before, the volume bonus, that one was the real problem.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about that.

**Distributor Owner:** The volume bonus for the 2025 season. Accrued, approved, everyone agreed. The credit note should come in thirty days, they told me. It came in, let me look. [Opens a folder on his laptop.] Approved in October. Credit note received in, here, end of February. Four months. And in the middle, their finance people called me about my overdue invoice. I said, I am not overdue, you owe me the bonus, take it off. They said, different department. My accountant almost had a heart attack.

**Interviewer:** Did you hold back payment?

**Distributor Owner:** Of course. Everybody does. If you owe me, I don't pay you until you pay me. It is not a credit problem. I have the money. It is a principle.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned the company's apps and reports. Do you use any of them?

**Distributor Owner:** They gave me a login to a portal two years ago. Statements, invoices. The password expired, I never reset it. My accountant downloads the statement once a month. The rest, the dashboards, the field app, that is for the company to watch its people. Not for me. My system is the messaging app. The territory manager sends me a photo of the price list, I send him a photo of my order. That works.

**Interviewer:** What does a good sales rep do differently?

**Distributor Owner:** The good one, I had one before this boy, he is now in another company. He came before the season and asked "what did you sell last season, by product, by dealer?" Not "what will you buy". He knew which of my dealers were strong. When a dealer was struggling he told me before I knew. He visited the dealers himself, not only me. And in a bad year he said "take less, we do it again in January." He lost some volume that year. I bought more from him the next two years. Trust is the bonus nobody pays for.

**Interviewer:** And this season?

**Distributor Owner:** They came again in August. Same presentation, new colours. I took half of what I took last year. The territory manager was not happy. I told him, show me that my dealers are buying, then I take more. This year the rain is normal so far, thank God. We will see.

**Interviewer:** If the company could give you one thing, what would it be?

**Distributor Owner:** Credit notes on time. And a rep who looks at what goes out of my door, not only at what comes in. The dealers tell me everything weeks before. If the company listened to the dealers, they would know too. That's all. Now come, I show you the warehouse. It is emptier this year. That is a good sign.

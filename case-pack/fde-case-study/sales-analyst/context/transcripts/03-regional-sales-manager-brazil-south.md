# Interview: Regional Sales Manager, Southern Brazil

- **Role:** Regional Sales Manager, Southern Brazil (territories BR-S01 to BR-S04)
- **Region:** Brazil South
- **Date:** 6 October 2026, video call
- **Interviewer:** Riklr FDE
- **Note:** Conducted in English. The manager's first language is Portuguese. The manager joined from a distributor's office in the interior. The connection dropped once.

---

**Interviewer:** Good morning. Can you hear me?

**Regional Sales Manager:** Bom dia, yes, I hear you. Sorry for the background. I am at a distributor in the interior, they gave me the meeting room. The wi-fi here is, how do you say, it has its moods. If I disappear, I come back.

**Interviewer:** No problem. Can you describe your region?

**Regional Sales Manager:** Four territories in the south. Rio Grande do Sul, Santa Catarina, Paraná. Soybean mainly, corn, some wheat in winter. About sixteen distributors. My season is the opposite of the US. We sell in July to October, farmers plant from September to December, harvest in March and April.

**Interviewer:** How was last year?

**Regional Sales Manager:** FY2025 was very good. We closed at a hundred and eleven percent of target. My team worked very hard in August and September to get there. Then this year was difficult. The drought came in November and December. Rain was maybe forty percent of normal. Farmers did not plant everything, and the ones who planted did not spray as much. So in February and March the distributors sent back a lot of product. Soybean seed, fungicides, insecticides.

**Interviewer:** How much came back?

**Regional Sales Manager:** I don't know the exact number. Finance knows. A lot. It hurt my net number very much. On gross I was around eighty percent of target this year. On net it looks terrible. And the returns came in the same fiscal year as my new targets, so I am punished twice.

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about one of those returns?

**Regional Sales Manager:** There is one I remember well. A distributor in the north of Rio Grande do Sul. Good man, I know him fifteen years. In September last year he took a very big order. Fungicide mostly. My territory manager called me, happy, "he took it all". It helped us close the year. Then in March he calls me directly, not the territory manager, me. He says "I have a warehouse full and the farmers did not spray. I need to send back." Three trucks. I went there myself. The boxes were still in the plastic from our factory. He was embarrassed. I was embarrassed. What can you do. It did not rain.

**Interviewer:** Could you have seen it earlier?

**Regional Sales Manager:** No. It was the weather. My distributors sell everything they buy, normally. They are good distributors. When it doesn't rain, nobody can sell.

**Interviewer:** How do you know what they've sold to dealers?

**Regional Sales Manager:** We ask. My territory managers visit and ask how the stock is moving. The distributors say "it's moving, it's moving". They want the early order discount, so they say yes to more product. Sometimes they are optimistic.

**Interviewer:** There's dealer scan data from the loyalty program. Do you use it?

**Regional Sales Manager:** That is for marketing. The dealers scan when they sell to the farmer, they get points. I have seen the report once or twice. I don't know if it's accurate. Not all dealers are in the program. And the dealers, some scan everything at the end of the month in one go, to get the points. So I don't know what it means.

**Interviewer:** How do you plan a quarter?

**Regional Sales Manager:** Sales ops gives me a target for the region. I split it across territories with my territory managers. Each one has a spreadsheet with their distributors. We look at last year, what the distributor says, the credit situation. Then I have a call every Monday with the four of them. We look at the dashboard and say who is behind.

**Interviewer:** Can you walk me through last Monday's call?

**Regional Sales Manager:** Seven thirty in the morning, before they go on the road. Forty-five minutes. I share my screen with the dashboard. Each territory manager says his number, his top three distributors, what is the problem. Then they also send me a photo of their spreadsheet in the group, because the dashboard does not have the orders they took on Friday on the messaging app. So I have the dashboard on one side and four photos on the other side and I make the total in my head. Then at the end I say "this one, go there, this one, call him". That's the call.

**Interviewer:** The group, is that a work channel?

**Regional Sales Manager:** It's a messaging group. Me and the four. There is another group with each distributor. The official tool is the team chat, but the territory managers do not open it on the road. Everything real happens in the messaging groups. The back office types the orders into the ERP from photos. Sometimes two days later.

**Interviewer:** Is the dashboard up to date?

**Regional Sales Manager:** It's from yesterday. There was a problem in August when we changed the ERP, but IT says it's fixed now.

**Interviewer:** How do you know it's from yesterday?

**Regional Sales Manager:** It says at the top, "updated" and the date of yesterday. Every morning.

**Interviewer:** Do the numbers on it match what your team...

**Regional Sales Manager:** Sorry, you... [connection lost]

**Regional Sales Manager:** Sorry, sorry. The router is in the warehouse and someone moved a pallet. Where were we?

**Interviewer:** The dashboard, and whether it matches what your territory managers report.

**Regional Sales Manager:** Ah. Not always. The territory managers always have more than the dashboard. But that is normal, they have the orders from the weekend. I never worried about it.

**Interviewer:** Did you use the old bot?

**Regional Sales Manager:** A few times. I asked in Portuguese and it did not understand. Then I asked in English and it gave me a number in reais that didn't match my dashboard. One of my territory managers told me he could see the numbers of other territories in the bot. That was strange. I stopped using it.

**Interviewer:** Did you report that?

**Regional Sales Manager:** I told the BI team. Someone wrote back "thank you, we will investigate". Nobody told me after. I also told my territory manager, don't play with it. He was curious about his colleague next door. Normal. But if a distributor sees that on his phone, it's a problem.

**Interviewer:** If there were an AI analyst for your team, what would you want it to do?

**Regional Sales Manager:** Help my territory managers decide where to go. They have big territories. Four, five hours of driving sometimes. If something could tell them "this distributor has stock problems, go there first", that is useful. Also receivables. Brazil has long terms, a hundred and fifty days, and some distributors pay late. I want to see it early.

**Interviewer:** How do you find out about late payments today?

**Regional Sales Manager:** The regional finance sends the aging report by email once a month. A big file. I take a screenshot of my part and send to the group. By the time I see it, the territory manager already knows, because the distributor already complained to him.

**Interviewer:** How do you feel about AI helping with planning?

**Regional Sales Manager:** Honestly? A little worried. If the computer makes the plan, what is my job? I have twenty years in this region. I know these people. A computer does not know that a distributor's son just took over the company and he is more aggressive. I don't want a machine telling the global head that my plan is wrong.

**Interviewer:** Has that happened, head office questioning the plan with data?

**Regional Sales Manager:** After the returns, yes. In the April review somebody from finance showed a slide with my returns in red, big. In front of everybody. Nobody asked me first. I had the explanation, the drought, but the slide was already on the screen. So when somebody says "the AI will show the global head where the problems are", I think of that slide.

**Interviewer:** What would make you comfortable?

**Regional Sales Manager:** If it is my tool. If I decide what the team sees and I can correct it. If it helps me in the Monday call, not replaces me in it.

**Interviewer:** What do your territory managers' days look like?

**Regional Sales Manager:** They start early, six or seven. Drive to a distributor, then to two or three dealers, sometimes a farmer meeting. Signal is bad in the countryside. They use the phone for everything, messages, photos, voice notes. They don't open the laptop until the evening, if at all. They speak Portuguese. Some speak a little Spanish. English, not really.

**Interviewer:** And head office tools in general. How are they received?

**Regional Sales Manager:** [Laughs.] Every year something new. The field app, the new CRM screen, the bot, a training portal with videos in English. The territory managers call it "mais uma coisa da matriz", one more thing from head office. They are polite in the training and then they go back to the messaging app. If it does not save them time in the truck, forget it.

**Interviewer:** Anything you'd ask us to be careful about?

**Regional Sales Manager:** Don't make a tool that shows my distributors' problems to everybody in the company. These are relationships. And please don't use last season to set this season's targets without thinking. Last season was the drought.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. I'll let you get back to your distributor.

**Regional Sales Manager:** Obrigado. He is waiting with the coffee. If you come to the south, I take you to see a real farm.

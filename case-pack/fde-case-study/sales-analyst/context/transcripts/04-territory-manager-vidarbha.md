# Interview: Territory Manager, Vidarbha

- **Role:** Territory Manager, Vidarbha (territory IN-W01)
- **Region:** India West and South
- **Date:** 7 October 2026, in person at a distributor's office, with a colleague translating from Marathi and Hindi
- **Interviewer:** Riklr FDE

---

**Territory Manager:** Please, sit. Have tea first. The distributor insists. He says the people from head office never drink the tea, they are always in a hurry.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. We're not in a hurry. Can you walk us through a normal day?

**Territory Manager:** I leave home at seven. First I go to one distributor. We check stock, his orders, his payment, and he complains about schemes. Then I go to dealers in the villages. Four, five, six dealers in a day, depending on the road. In kharif season sometimes a farmer meeting in the evening. I come home at eight or nine.

**Interviewer:** What about Mondays? Is Monday different?

**Territory Manager:** Monday is the worst. Monday morning, nine thirty, there is the call with the regional manager and all the territory managers of the area. Six of us. I am usually in the bike or the car, so I join on the phone with one earphone. He asks each one, how much order this week, what is pending. I say the number from my notebook. Then after the call, finance sends the overdue list for the area on the messaging group. Every Monday. So Monday afternoon I am calling distributors about payment, not selling.

**Interviewer:** How many distributors do you cover?

**Territory Manager:** Five distributors. Under them, maybe sixty dealers I know well. More that I don't know.

**Interviewer:** What crops?

**Territory Manager:** Cotton is the main. Soybean. Some tur dal, chilli, vegetables near the town. Cotton seed sells in May and June before the monsoon. Then insecticides in July, August. Rabi is smaller here.

**Interviewer:** What tools do you use?

**Territory Manager:** The phone. I use the field app to check in at the distributor, because my manager checks that. I put orders in the app sometimes, but mostly the distributor sends the order on a messaging app and I forward it. For my plan, the regional office sends a spreadsheet every month. I don't open it on the phone, it's too small. I keep the numbers in my head and in a notebook.

**Interviewer:** Could I see the notebook?

**Territory Manager:** [Laughs. Takes it out of his bag.] Here. Every page is one distributor. This side, what he ordered, the date. This side, what credit he is waiting for. Here, in red, what he owes. I update it in the evening. My wife says I talk to the notebook more than to her.

**Interviewer:** The red numbers. Where do they come from?

**Territory Manager:** From the finance list on Monday. I copy them by hand. Sometimes from a screenshot the regional office sends of the dashboard. And sometimes the distributor tells me himself, and his number is different from finance. Then I write both.

**Interviewer:** What does the distributor complain about?

**Territory Manager:** Scheme credits. Always. He bought for the kharif scheme, he was promised a credit note, the credit note does not come. Some are from April, May. Six months. So he says, "Until my credit comes, I will not pay your invoice." Then finance calls me and says, "Why is your distributor overdue?" I am in the middle. I don't have the list of which credits are pending. I call the regional office and they say they will check.

**Interviewer:** When did that last happen?

**Territory Manager:** This morning. Before you came. Same distributor, this one, the one who gave us tea. [Translator: He is saying the distributor raised it again just now in the other room.] He has a list on his computer of every scheme. His list is better than ours. He showed me three credits from the summer that he says are not received.

*[The territory manager's phone rings. He steps out for about two minutes.]*

**Territory Manager:** Sorry. That was the regional finance. Exactly what I said. They want to know why this distributor has not paid the August invoice. I told them, ask the scheme team about his credits. They said they will check. Every time, "we will check".

**Interviewer:** How often does this come up?

**Territory Manager:** Every visit. Every single visit. It's the first ten minutes.

**Interviewer:** How many of your five distributors are in this situation now?

**Territory Manager:** Let me think. This one, yes. The one near the highway, yes, he is the loudest. The others, I am not sure. One of them pays first and fights later, he is old style. I have to check the notebook.

**Interviewer:** Did you use the old sales bot?

**Territory Manager:** Once or twice. My manager said to try it. I asked in Marathi what is the outstanding for my distributor. It said sorry, it does not understand. I tried in Hindi, same. I don't type English fast. After that I didn't use it.

**Interviewer:** Did others in your area try it?

**Territory Manager:** Everybody tried once, because the manager said. One colleague asked in English and waited, waited, then the network went. He said "it is thinking more than me." After two weeks nobody talked about it. In the group we made jokes.

**Interviewer:** If you had an assistant on your phone, what would you ask it?

**Territory Manager:** Which credit notes are pending for this distributor, and when they will come. That first. Then, how much did this distributor buy this season versus last season. Which dealers have stopped buying. And the stock. If the distributor has too much stock of one product, I should not push more, because at the end of the season he will return it and then everybody is angry.

**Interviewer:** Does that happen?

**Territory Manager:** Yes. At quarter end, the pressure comes. "Push, push, close the target." The distributor takes it as a favour. Then after the season, return. In my area not so big. In Brazil I heard it was very big last year.

**Interviewer:** Can you give me an example from here?

**Territory Manager:** Two years back the monsoon came late. Very late. One distributor in the next taluka, not mine, had taken a lot of cotton seed and insecticide at the June quarter end. When the forecast said dry spell, he got scared. He sold everything to his dealers at a discount, very fast, before the dry weeks. Dumped it. The dealers were stuck with it, not him. Then the dealers did not pay him, and he did not pay us. It took one full year to clean. My regional manager still talks about it.

**Interviewer:** Would you use voice?

**Territory Manager:** Yes, if it understands Marathi. I send voice notes all day. But the network in the villages is weak. Sometimes nothing. It should work in the morning before I leave, or at the distributor where there is wi-fi.

**Interviewer:** What would worry you about an AI tool?

**Territory Manager:** If it tells my manager I did not go where it said. Some days I cannot go. A dealer calls with a problem, I go there. A road is broken. If it becomes another thing to check me, I will not like it. If it helps me, I will use it.

**Interviewer:** Has that happened with the field app?

**Territory Manager:** With the check-in, yes. One month the report went to the zonal head showing who checked in less. My name was there. That month I had two farmer meetings and a crop failure complaint in one village. None of that is a "check-in". Nobody asked me why. So now I check in everywhere, even when I am only passing.

**Interviewer:** How do you know which dealers are actually selling to farmers?

**Territory Manager:** I see their shop. If the shop is full of our bags in July, it's not selling. Some dealers scan in the loyalty app, but many don't. In my territory maybe one in four, one in five. The bigger ones scan.

**Interviewer:** How did your territory do last year?

**Territory Manager:** I think good. My manager said eighty-something percent, close to ninety. The dashboard is always a little behind what I know, but in India it's okay, it's from yesterday.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything the regional office could give you that would help today?

**Territory Manager:** One page for each distributor before I visit. What he bought, what he owes, what credits are pending, which dealers are slow. In Marathi, or at least simple. That's all. I don't need a robot. I need the page. Like my notebook, but correct.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. That's very clear.

**Territory Manager:** And tell them about the credit notes. Please. Tell them it is not only me. Ask any territory manager in Maharashtra, they will say the same thing.

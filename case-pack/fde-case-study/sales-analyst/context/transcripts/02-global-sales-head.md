# Interview: Global Sales Head

- **Role:** Global Sales Head
- **Region:** Global, covers North America, Brazil, Europe and India
- **Date:** 5 October 2026
- **Interviewer:** Riklr FDE

---

**Global Sales Head:** Sorry I'm late. The Europe call ran over. Bonus season. Everybody suddenly has a reason why their number should be higher.

**Interviewer:** No problem. How did FY2026 go?

**Global Sales Head:** Good year overall. We grew about eleven percent on sell-in, that's what my dashboard shows. India and North America were strong. Brazil was tough because of the drought in the south. Europe was fine.

**Interviewer:** Which dashboard is that?

**Global Sales Head:** The global sell-in dashboard. The one with the big number at the top. Let me pull it up. Here. You see? Big number, growth next to it, then the four regions underneath. Finance will tell you a lower number, they always do. They take out returns and credits and convert everything at their own rates. I run the business on sell-in because that's what the teams control and that's what targets are set on.

**Interviewer:** Does that difference ever cause trouble?

**Global Sales Head:** Every month. Let me give you the best example. The Q2 review in April. I had the regional managers on, the finance controller on, and the CEO dropped in for twenty minutes. I said India was on track. The controller said India was short. Same quarter, same region. We spent the whole twenty minutes the CEO was there arguing about that one number. Turns out I was on order date in rupees and finance was on invoice date in dollars, minus credits. We were both right. The CEO left saying "sort out your numbers". That was the only thing he took from the meeting.

**Interviewer:** Was it sorted out?

**Global Sales Head:** We agreed to put both numbers on the slide. So now every slide has two numbers and people pick the one they like. That's not sorted, that's a truce.

**Interviewer:** How do you plan a quarter today?

**Global Sales Head:** Sales ops sends targets. Each territory manager builds a plan in a spreadsheet. The regional managers review them in the monthly meeting. Then I look at the roll-up. Honestly a lot of it is experience. A good territory manager knows which distributor will take another truck of product and which one won't. The data helps but it's scattered. Some of it is in the dashboards, some in the CRM, some in spreadsheets that live on people's laptops.

**Interviewer:** Can I see one of those spreadsheets?

**Global Sales Head:** I don't have the territory ones. I have the roll-up. Hang on. This one. Six tabs, one per sub-region, each pasted in from a different file. You see the colours are different on each tab? That's because each regional manager uses their own template. The Brazil South one has a column called "feeling". I'm not joking. Green, yellow, red. It's actually the most useful column in the file.

**Interviewer:** What would you want from an AI analyst?

**Global Sales Head:** Forecasting, first. I want to know where we'll land before the quarter ends, not after. Second, I want to know where to put the team's time. We have thirty-four territory managers and they can't see every distributor every month. Which ones matter this month? Third, I want fewer surprises from finance. Receivables, credit notes, returns. Those all hit my number after the fact.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned surprises. Returns?

**Global Sales Head:** Returns are small for us, a couple of percent. Brazil South last season was an exception. We shipped heavily before the season, then it didn't rain, and a lot came back in February and March. That was weather. Nobody controls weather.

**Interviewer:** Did anyone see it coming?

**Global Sales Head:** Not that I know of. The distributors kept ordering, so on our side it looked fine.

**Interviewer:** When did you first hear about it?

**Global Sales Head:** A phone call from the regional manager. February, I think, early February. He said "we have a problem, the distributors want to send product back". By then it was already on trucks. The finance controller sent me the number a month later. I remember thinking, that's a lot of product for one sub-region. But the drought was real. I saw the pictures. Dry fields, soybeans the height of my hand.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the old bot.

**Global Sales Head:** I wanted it to work. I really did. I asked it for Brazil sales one morning before a call with the regional team. It gave me a number that was off by a mile. I checked the dashboard and it wasn't even close. After that I never trusted it. If I can't trust one number, I can't trust any of them. My teams felt the same way. It was also slow. You'd ask something and go get coffee.

**Interviewer:** What did you do on that call?

**Global Sales Head:** I'd already put the number in my opening notes. I caught it about five minutes before the call because something felt off, and I opened the dashboard to check. If I hadn't, I'd have told the Brazil team they were way ahead when they were behind. Imagine that. Their regional manager would never have let me forget it.

**Interviewer:** Did you find out why the number was wrong?

**Global Sales Head:** Something about which sales it counted. And currencies. I don't know the details. I just know my people stopped using it.

**Interviewer:** If we build something new, why would they use it when they stopped using the old one?

**Global Sales Head:** That's the right question. It would have to be right, every time, and it would have to save them time on something they already do. Not another place to look.

**Interviewer:** How do the regional managers feel about this?

**Global Sales Head:** Nervous, some of them. There's a story going around that the AI will do the planning and we won't need regional managers. That's not my plan. But I'll be honest, if the AI can tell a territory manager which distributor to visit, I want to know when they ignore it. Maybe we tie part of the incentive to following the recommendations. That would get adoption fast.

**Interviewer:** How would the territory managers feel about that?

**Global Sales Head:** They wouldn't like it. But they don't like most things at first. When we made the field app check-in mandatory they hated it for six months. Now they use it. Mostly. Some of them check in from the petrol station near the distributor, I know that. Sorry, one second. [Phone buzzes.] It's Europe again. I'll call him back.

**Interviewer:** What does a good territory manager do differently from an average one?

**Global Sales Head:** They know their distributors' stock. They know who has cash and who doesn't. They know when a scheme credit is late and the distributor is angry about it. They visit the right people before the season, not during it. Most of that is in their head, not in any system.

**Interviewer:** Where does that knowledge live, practically?

**Global Sales Head:** Messaging groups. Every territory manager has a group with each of his distributors, sometimes with the distributor's sons and the accountant too. Orders come in there as photos of handwritten lists. Payment promises come in there. Complaints come in there. None of it reaches us. When a territory manager leaves, all of that leaves with him. We lost a very good one in Brazil Center last year and his replacement spent three months rebuilding relationships the company had paid for.

**Interviewer:** What about the loyalty scan data? The dealer scans.

**Global Sales Head:** That's a marketing program. Dealers scan packs to get points. I don't look at it much. Coverage is patchy, especially in India. I'm not sure what it tells me about my number. The loyalty team sends a monthly deck. It's mostly about how many dealers enrolled.

**Interviewer:** How would you judge whether a new analyst works?

**Global Sales Head:** If my regional managers use it in the monthly review instead of arguing about whose number is right, that's a win. If territory managers hit their targets more often, even better. But I've seen pilots before. Someone always shows a chart where the pilot group grew faster. The old bot had one of those. I didn't believe it then.

**Interviewer:** Why not?

**Global Sales Head:** Because the pilot was India, and India always grows in the kharif quarter. You could have piloted a new coffee machine and shown the same chart.

**Interviewer:** Did you say that at the time?

**Global Sales Head:** I said it in the corridor. Not in the meeting. The CDO had his slide and the budget was already half approved. You pick your battles. I'm saying it to you now.

**Interviewer:** Anything else we should know?

**Global Sales Head:** Talk to the field. Not just the managers. Our territory managers in India and Brazil don't work in English and they don't sit at desks. If it doesn't work for them, it doesn't work. And one more thing. Don't make me another dashboard. I have enough places to look already.

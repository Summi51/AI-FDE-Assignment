# Interview: Chief Digital Officer

- **Role:** Chief Digital Officer
- **Region:** Global, based at head office
- **Date:** 5 October 2026
- **Interviewer:** Riklr FDE

---

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time. I know the first week after year end is busy.

**Chief Digital Officer:** It's chaos. Finance is closing, sales is arguing about bonuses, and I have three steering committees this week. But this one matters to me, so. Give me one second, let me put my phone face down. There. Go.

**Interviewer:** Can you start with what you want from this project?

**Chief Digital Officer:** Sure. I want an analyst that never sleeps. Anyone in sales, from the global head down to a territory manager in a pickup truck, should be able to ask a question and get an answer from our data in seconds. Not a dashboard. Not a ticket to the BI team that comes back in a week. Just ask and get the answer.

**Interviewer:** When did someone last wait a week for an answer?

**Chief Digital Officer:** Last month. The sales head wanted to know which distributors had stopped buying in Europe. Simple question. It went into the BI queue, came back eight days later as a spreadsheet with forty tabs, and by then the meeting had happened. That is the pattern. The BI team are good people. They are just a queue.

**Interviewer:** What kind of questions should the analyst take?

**Chief Digital Officer:** Anything. That's the point. How did we do in Brazil last month. Which distributors are behind on payments. What should I push in my territory next quarter. Why is Europe down. If I have to list the questions in advance then I've built another dashboard, and we have forty of those already.

**Interviewer:** How many of the forty get opened?

**Chief Digital Officer:** Honestly? I asked for the view counts last year. Maybe eight are used every week. The rest are monuments. Someone wanted one, we built it, they moved roles. Sales has theirs, finance has theirs. You will see.

**Interviewer:** Where would people use the analyst?

**Chief Digital Officer:** Three places. First, the team chat tool, because that's where the office lives. Second, in meetings. I want it to join the monthly regional review, listen, and when someone says "I think we're ahead on fungicides" it pulls the real number. Third, and this is the big one, voice in the field app. Our territory managers drive all day. They won't type. They should be able to talk to it.

**Interviewer:** Have you sat in one of those monthly reviews recently?

**Chief Digital Officer:** I sat in the Brazil one in August. Two hours. I would say forty minutes of it was people reading numbers off different screens and disagreeing. One regional manager had a screenshot of a dashboard on his phone. Another had his own spreadsheet. The finance person had a third number. Nobody was wrong exactly, but nobody decided anything for forty minutes. That's what I want to kill.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned access. How should that work?

**Chief Digital Officer:** It has to follow the permissions we already have. A territory manager sees their territory. A regional manager sees their region. That's non-negotiable. Legal and finance will kill it otherwise.

**Interviewer:** There was a sales bot before. What happened to it?

**Chief Digital Officer:** Yes, we launched one about eighteen months ago. A vendor built it on the language models that were around then. Honestly the models weren't good enough. It struggled with complex questions and it was slow. At the peak we had hundreds of people using it every week, so the demand is clearly there. The pilot even showed that territories using it grew much faster than the rest. Then usage dropped off. I think people got bored of the limitations.

**Interviewer:** Hundreds every week. Do you remember where that figure came from?

**Chief Digital Officer:** The launch report, and the vendor's monthly update. There was a chart going up and to the right. I presented it to the executive committee. I don't have the exact number in my head, but it was hundreds. The BI lead will have the detail. She keeps everything.

**Interviewer:** And the growth in pilot territories. What did that look like?

**Chief Digital Officer:** Strong. The pilot territories were up a lot quarter on quarter and the others were down. That was the slide that got us the budget for the full rollout. Some people in sales were sceptical, they always are. But the numbers were the numbers.

**Interviewer:** What would make a new one different?

**Chief Digital Officer:** The models are far better now. They can write SQL, they can reason, they can speak any language. A lot of the problems we had were model problems, and those are mostly solved. We also have a proper cloud warehouse now. Everything is in one place.

**Interviewer:** Sorry, can I check one thing. Was the old bot on the same warehouse?

**Chief Digital Officer:** Yes. Well. Same warehouse, it was newer then. Fewer tables. The point is the data is there. What we didn't have was a model that could understand a messy question.

**Interviewer:** How would you know if the new one is working?

**Chief Digital Officer:** Two ways. Usage, first. If people use it every day, it's useful. Then sales. I'd like to roll it out to half the territories for a quarter and compare them with the other half. If the AI territories sell more, we have our case. That's what I'll take to the executive committee.

**Interviewer:** How would you pick which half?

**Chief Digital Officer:** I hadn't got that far. Probably the regions most keen to try. India was very keen last time. Or we let the regional managers volunteer. Does it matter much?

**Interviewer:** We can come back to it. And if it works?

**Chief Digital Officer:** Then it goes to the other business units. We have an animal health business and a specialty nutrition business with the same setup. Same warehouse, same chat tool. I see this as a platform, not a one-off.

**Interviewer:** Who in sales is most excited about it?

**Chief Digital Officer:** The global sales head is supportive, though he's been burned by the old bot. The territory managers will love it once they see it. The middle layer is harder. Some regional managers like their spreadsheets. Change is always hard for that layer. But the field will pull it through.

**Interviewer:** What makes you think the field will pull it?

**Chief Digital Officer:** They're young, a lot of them. They live on their phones. They send voice notes to distributors all day on the messaging apps. If they can send a voice note to us and get an answer back, why wouldn't they? I'll admit there's some fatigue. We gave them the field app, then a new CRM screen, then a training portal, then the bot. Every year head office sends them something new. I understand why they roll their eyes. This one has to feel different.

**Interviewer:** What worries you most?

**Chief Digital Officer:** Speed of delivery, honestly. I've promised the executive committee something they can see by the end of the calendar year. And I don't want another bot that gives a wrong number in front of the CEO. That happened once with the old one.

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me what happened?

**Chief Digital Officer:** It was a quarterly business review. The CEO likes live demos, he thinks slides hide things. So I said, ask it anything. Someone asked for global sales for the year. It thought for a long time, maybe half a minute, everyone watching the screen. Then it came back with a number that was something like fifteen times too high. Billions. The CEO laughed, which was worse than if he'd been angry. The finance controller didn't laugh. It was a bad day for me.

**Interviewer:** Do you know why the number was wrong?

**Chief Digital Officer:** The BI team said something about currencies. That's exactly the kind of thing a better model should just handle.

**Interviewer:** Some of the commercial team described the problem differently on our first call. They talked about planning the quarter on spreadsheets and gut feel. How do you see that?

**Chief Digital Officer:** It's the same problem. If they can ask the analyst anything, they'll plan better. I don't want to narrow this to a planning tool. If we build one narrow use case we'll be back here in a year building the next one. The value is in the breadth.

**Interviewer:** Anything you'd rule out?

**Chief Digital Officer:** I don't want it making decisions for people. And I don't want a six-month data cleanup project before anything ships. The data is good enough. The BI team will tell you there are issues, and there are always issues, but we can't wait for perfect.

**Interviewer:** What kind of issues has she raised with you?

**Chief Digital Officer:** Oh, the usual list. Definitions, codes, Brazil had something with the ERP move in August. She sends me a status note every month. I'll be honest, I read the top paragraph. If something is on fire she calls me, and she hasn't called.

**Interviewer:** Last question. If you could see one thing in a demo, what would it be?

**Chief Digital Officer:** A territory manager in India asks something out loud in their own language, in the field, and gets the right answer for their territory in a few seconds. If I see that, I'm sold.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. That's very helpful.

**Chief Digital Officer:** Talk to the sales head next. And the BI lead. She'll give you the warehouse access. Oh, and go to the field. I'll get someone in India to set up a visit. Don't just talk to people in this building.
